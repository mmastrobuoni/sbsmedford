Our purpose

More “aha!”
in every school day.

In spring 2026, a group of Medford parents came together to advocate for responsible technology use and more opportunities for active and outdoor time in our schools. We became an official chapter of Schools Beyond Screens, a national coalition of parents and educators calling for classroom technology policies based on evidence.

Our chapter includes members from all four Medford elementary schools and both middle schools. We’re partnering with MPS leadership in a collaborative spirit to help create the best learning environment for every student.

We are not anti-tech; we are pro-learning.

Our group’s goals

More play.
Thoughtful technology.

These are the changes our chapter is advocating for in Medford Public Schools.

What we’re working on now

  1. Screen-free lunch, snack, and recess

    No individual screens or screens shown to the whole class during these times, so students can socialize.

  2. A choice about extra technology

    Allow parents and caregivers to opt their children out of supplemental technology, meaning technology not used for testing, when reasonable.

  3. Evidence of value and safety

    Use apps and technology only when independent, peer-reviewed research shows educational value and data security.

  4. Clear information for families

    Keep parents and caregivers fully informed about their student’s app use on school-issued devices.

  5. Recess built around play

    Hold recess outdoors whenever possible. Keep indoor and outdoor recess focused on play and physical activity. Support these goals by raising funds for recess supplies and equipment.

What we’re working toward

  1. Clear limits by grade

    Set intentional limits on devices and screen time in each grade, with more room for learning led by teachers and students.

  2. Limits on generative AI

    No student use of generative AI in kindergarten through grade 8, with limits for grades 9–12.

  3. Shared classroom devices

    Move away from a device for every student and return to a shared cart of devices in each classroom to support these goals.

  4. A school day free of personal devices

    Ban electronic devices, including cell phones and smart watches, from the start to the end of the school day in kindergarten through grade 12, with the access protections described below.

  5. More active time beyond school

    Promote ways for adults and children to disconnect from technology, get outdoors, and be active outside school.

Access matters. We support devices and tools that help students with disabilities and language barriers communicate, access instruction, and participate in the classroom, as well as technology required by Individualized Education Programs (IEPs).

Is this about classroom technology or phones?

Both. Our goals cover school-issued devices, apps, classroom screen time, generative AI, cell phones, and smart watches. We also advocate for more active and outdoor time.

Does this mean removing every screen?

We want intentional technology use, with clear limits and evidence of educational value. Our goals include shared classroom devices and screen-free meals and recess. We support technology that helps students access instruction and communicate.

What about accessibility and assistive technology?

We support devices and tools that help students with disabilities and language barriers communicate, access instruction, and participate in class. We also support technology required by IEPs.

Who can come to a meeting?

Our monthly meetings are open to all. We usually meet on the second Tuesday of each month, from 7–8 pm. Join our Facebook group for meeting updates.

Is this part of Medford Public Schools?

We are a community group and an official chapter of the national Schools Beyond Screens organization. We collaborate with MPS leaders, but we are independent of the school district and the City of Medford.
